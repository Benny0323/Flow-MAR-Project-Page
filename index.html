Abstract

Overview

Computed tomography (CT) is a widely used imaging modality for clinical diagnosis. However, metallic implants severely attenuate X-rays and corrupt the projection data, producing streak and shading artifacts that degrade image quality and impede accurate diagnosis. In recent years, generative models—especially diffusion models—have achieved remarkable success in image restoration and solving ill-posed inverse problems, motivating their application to metal artifact reduction. However, existing diffusion-based MAR methods are limited by slow sampling efficiency from noise initialization, or fail to fully leverage prior information and preserve anatomical fidelity. To address this issue, we present Flow-MAR, a one-step consistency flow framework with projection-aware refinement for CT MAR. We explicitly model a rectified flow paradigm from prior-guided to clean images in latent space, avoiding the training burden of diffusion models in high-dimensional image space and the pronounced difficulty of direct restoration from artifact-affected images in a few steps. A consistency learning mechanism with self-distillation and proposed alignment loss further enables one-step generation. To better restore fine anatomical details and mitigate structural inconsistency, we incorporate a residual refinement module with iterative projection-aware consistency to increase physical plausibility. Experiments show that Flow-MAR not only achieves meaningful improvements on simulated datasets but also demonstrates efficiency on clinical scenarios and downstream tasks.

Method

Architecture

Flow-MAR consists of two core components: a rectified flow generator that maps prior-guided images to clean images in latent space via one-step inference, and a projection-aware residual refinement module that enforces physical consistency through iterative projection constraints.

1

Rectified Flow in Latent Space

We model a straight flow from prior-guided latent to clean latent, avoiding high-dimensional image-space training and enabling efficient one-step inference via consistency distillation.

2

Consistency Learning with Self-Distillation

A self-distillation mechanism paired with an alignment loss ensures that multi-step and one-step trajectories produce consistent outputs, collapsing inference to a single generation step.

3

Projection-Aware Residual Refinement

Iterative projection consistency constraints on non-metal trajectories suppress hallucination, while a residual refinement module repairs secondary artifacts and restores tissue detail.
